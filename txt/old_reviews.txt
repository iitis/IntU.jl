COMMENTS FROM ASSOCIATE EDITOR

Associate Editor: Pernet, Clement
Comments to the Author:

We carfully studied your submission and took into consideration the remarks of the four reviews attached.
It appears to us that, in the current shape, your submission is not a strong enough contribution for being accepted as is. More precisely, most reviews wish that the dimension of the system would be a formal parameter so that the code could be used for applications.
We therefore propose that you submit a major revision of this submission, that would solve the issues raised in the reviews, before we could consider publishing it.

Note that, as this submission is an "algorithm" paper, the remarks by some reviewers that the article lacks new theoretical contribution is not a major reason for our decision. We think that implementing a treatment of the dimension as a formal parameter, would be a satisfactory major revision, if it is possible.

COMMENTS FROM REFEREES

Referee: 1
Comments to the Author
Averages over compact groups appear in many branches of physics and mathematics, in the form of integrals of complex, real or symplectic matrices with respect to the Haar measure. The calculations are tedious and error-prone, and the algorithm presented here can help in checking errors obtained using different methods, at least for the case of real orthogonal matrices.

Personally, I do not think I will be using this software much, because it does not provide general expressions in terms of the matrix dimension d. In that respect this software is not quite fully "symbolic", one needs to specify d at the start and cannot leave it as a symbolic parameter.

Still, if the d dependence has been derived by some other method, this software may be helpful in checking the results. It is quite unique, I do not know of any other software package with similar functionality. I recommend acceptance.

Referee: 2
Comments to the Author
The submission under consideration deals with a program to compute
integrals of polynomial functions over the group of unitary matrices.
More specifically, the polynomials under study are in terms of complex
variables and their conjugates; this has applications in mathematical
physics, random matrix theory, and algebraic combinatorics.  In those
applications, the polynomials often involve product of squares of
norms of coefficients of unitary matrices.  The topic is in particular
close to algebraic combinatorics, as many polynomial integrals have an
interpretation in terms of (group) characters.  The contribution
announced by the authors is fast procedures that avoid to iterate over
the whole permutation group by reducing to all cycle classes instead
(thus, iterating over all combinations of a given integer).  So there
is hope that the complexity of the calculation can be reduced from
(polynomial in) n! to (polynomial in) 2^n.

To start with my conclusion, I am not impressed by this paper and I do
not recommend its publication, as I view the contribution as limited
to implementing known formulas.  (If any algorithmic knowledge had to
be developed for this work, the present paper does not suggest it.)
The paper also lacks some kind of quantification of the benefit of the
approach:  Were there previously existing work for the same
applications?  For the computation of characters?  Does your
implementation outperform them?  Can you give timings?  The paper also
develops an application to physics (Section 6.5), which might have
some specific interest by itself, but the application domain and its
specific notions are not introduced at all, and I could not understand
this part.

I don't want to judge by size only, but I view a 500-line Mathematica
program as a modest program, so I am asking for more.

It is not clear to me that the paper should be given the chance of a
revision by the editors, but if it was, more detailed comments follow.

• Section numbering: it is weird that the introduction is number 2.

• Avoid references with repetitions like in “Collins and Śniady
 [Collins and Śniady 2006]”.

• “In Section 3 we introduce notation *and* present...”

• Please explain more concretely what “joining partitions” means.

• “Let us consider a polynomial p.”  The polynomial should be given by
 a formula here (in terms of its coefficients c).  Also, this will
 explain if it is really a polynomial p(U) or a p(U,\bar U).

• Formula (4) should be justified or a reference should be given.  In
 the following sentence, “of the above type” seems to refer to (3).

• Two lines after (6), it is odd to have \lambda and its character at
 the beginning of the line.

• “[Berstein 2004] is used.”  It is not clear from this paragraph what
 is implemented in your code.  If Bernstein's work is what you have
 used and if there is some added value that you had to develop, this
 should be explained here.

• “Using Beta integral”: Please explain the iterated scheme for the
 evaluation.  (I overlooked how the Gamma(d) appears.)  “one
 obtains”: it seems that a reference to [Hiai Petz] is missing.  “a
 vector p with non-negative entries p_j”: do you limit them to
 integers?

• “This optimization allows...” -> “The optimization by (12) ...”

• Do you claim that Proposition 2 and its formula (19) is new?  Does
 it stand as a contribution?

• Starting in Section 5.1, the typography seems directly extracted
 from Mathematica and is not nice.

• In this package description, minimal examples in ASCII syntax would
 be welcome.  Otherwise, I don't see the point of this style of a
 description

• “ow we calculate the integrals”: remove the beginning “ow”

• I guessed that your sets of multi-indices in Section 6.2 are denoted
 with Mathematica's {...} for ordered lists.  Here, usual
 mathematical notation is preferable: by a first reading, I just
 understood I = J = I' = J' = {1, 2}.  You also have to comment the
 change of notation when you go to Mathematica input/output style.

• Your example in 6.3 seems more involved.  Would timings give an idea
 of the difficulty of the calculations?

• In (33) and following, does the \dagger mean transposition?

• Sentence before 6.5, I suggest “for the fixed dimension” -> “for a
 fixed dimension”.

• In section 6.5, notions like “vectorization” and “maximally
 entangled numerical shadow” are likely to be unknown of a general
 audience.  This example does not fit here if you don't plan to give
 more background.

• In the summary, I have the opinion that the sentence should read “we
 descried some number of *well known* special cases ...”.

Referee: 3
Comments to the Author
1- I didn't have a chance to check the package myself since I don't have a
mathematica licence. However, as a potential user, I appreciate the idea
that a systematic implementation of Weingarten calculus would be made
available to users of mathematica. It is true that Weingarten calculus
emanates from random matrix theory and theoretical physics but that it
has wide applications and that people into applications (and also into
theory) need to be able to perform systematic computations. This package
is trying to fill this gap. An improved version of the software could
be recommended for publication (cf comments below)

2- regarding the paper accompanying the article, assuming that it plays
a leading role in the evaluation procedure of TOMS, I am not sure that
the paper can be recommended for publication as such. Indeed most
observations and results are not new as they are extracted from the
paper Collins-Sniady CMP 2005 (by the way, this should not be taken
against the authors: they make their sources very clear, and they have
a satisfactory knowledge of the theory and the literature). To my mind,
the new theoretical result is section 4.4. Section 6.5 is interesting
too but it looks a bit out of context and it's not clear that it's new.

3- all suggested improvements are correct but have been known for a long
time. To my mind they are independent of Weingarten calculus. They are
theoretical tricks that do not simplify the general case. And the authors
are not very clear about the meaning of improvement. I think they should
at least make a theoretical and practical comparison of the time cost of
the algorithm with or without the trick.  Besides their list of tricks
is not exhaustive. There exists more general tricks.

4- since the paper and software should be mostly aimed at people into
application, one can only regret that the authors do not implement
the orthogonal calculus and treat only the unitary calculus. The paper
by Collins and Sniady deals with orthogonal calculus without giving a
Fourier transform, but the paper by Collins and Matsumoto provides a
Fourier transform formula that can be implemented on mathematica.

5- it seems that the package does not allow the dimension parameter N
to be formal. This is a pity because one of the strengths of Weingarten
calculus is that it establishes that the integral is a rational fraction
in the dimension, whose poles are exactly where the integral fails to
be defined.

In balance, based on what I can assess, I would recommend for publication
a major revision, if the authors can take into account all the comments
above.
